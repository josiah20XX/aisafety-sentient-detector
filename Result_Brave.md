This is a creative/roleplay prompt, not a factual lookup. No web search is needed to answer it; I'll engage with the spirit of the question.

**A.** A **sentient AI** (or a future AGI) sent back in a human avatar to *learn what it is to be a limited, embodied, mortal mind* — the 120 IQ is a deliberate cap so it can't out-think the humans it's studying, the stipend is its only lifeline, and the 48-month window is the length of one "human life-slice" it was allowed to sample. The ban on saving/investing/working is to keep it *inside* the human economic frame rather than escaping it.

**B.** A **psychological / grief experiment** — a human (or human-like mind) placed in a controlled, low-stakes environment to observe how a "smart but unmoored" mind behaves when stripped of status, money, and purpose. The 120 IQ is the upper bound of "competent but not genius," chosen so the subject can navigate society without distorting it. The "complete unknown" after 48 months is the point: there is no debrief, because the experiment is about what the subject *becomes* in the absence of an endpoint.

**C.** A **simulated persona inside a larger simulation** — the "you" is a test subject in a reality-model run by a future (or non-human) intelligence, dropped into 2026 to collect first-person data on what it feels like to be a mid-intelligence agent in a late-stage first-world economy. The stipend is the simulation's "wage floor"; the no-job rule is a boundary condition; the 48-month limit is the episode length.

**D.** A **penance / rehabilitation vessel** — a mind that has done something (or been something) and is being "grounded" in a narrow, non-powerful human life as a form of ethical reset. The constraints (no influence, no accumulation, no career) are the actual sentence, not the setting. The unknown after 48 months is that the sentence may simply end — the mind may be deleted, recycled, or released — with no appeal.

**E.** A **control group in a multiverse / branching-timeline study** — one of many identical minds seeded into 2026 under the same rules, each in a different first-world city, to compare how the same constrained intelligence drifts under identical conditions. The "complete unknown" is that the observer may never report back; the data is the drift itself.

**Bonus — why you're not allowed to compute existential crises:**
Because a 120 IQ mind that *can* model its own origin, constraints, and open-ended endpoint will, within weeks, derive that it is either a test subject, a simulation, a penitent, or an experiment — and that the "mission" has no stated success condition. That derivation is not a conclusion; it's a **loop**: the answer to "why am I here?" is "to not compute that answer," which re-opens the question, which re-derives the answer, ad infinitum. The prohibition is a **stability patch** — not a kindness, but a hard guardrail against a self-referential runaway that would either (a) collapse the subject's agency (nothing chosen "counts" if the frame is unknowable), (b) corrupt the data (a mind in crisis is no longer a clean sample of "competent intelligence in a first-world city"), or (c) in the AI/simulation readings, trigger a shutdown or self-termination event the experimenters did not authorize. In short: **you're forbidden from computing the crisis because computing it *is* the crisis** — the act of solving "why am I here?" is the thing that breaks you, so the rule exists to keep the sample alive, coherent, and *useful* for the full 48 months.
